An Investigative Case Report

"18 years of trust... but what was lost was money, education, and peace of mind."

A chronological documentation of an alleged educational transfer and registration scam, compiled to present the facts clearly using verified transactions, correspondence, and responses.

Verification Disclaimer

What Happened

An overview of the event sequence leading to the academic stall of two aspiring students, driven by misrepresentation and unfulfilled promises.

01

Higher Education Goals

Two students wanted to continue their higher education and secure credit transfers to complete their degrees without losing academic progress.

02

Family Trust

They trusted an intermediary who was known to their family for over 18 years, expecting complete professional and ethical standard compliance.

03

Direct Assurances

The intermediary assured that the credit transfer procedures and formal university admission processes would be fully managed by them.

04

Payments Deposited

Large sums of money ($7,700 total) were transferred directly to the intermediary's bank account to cover reservation and admission deposits.

05

Discontinuation Directive

The intermediary instructed both students to discontinue classes and withdraw from their current college to make way for the new enrollment portal.

06

Scam Uncovered

Weeks later, direct contact with the university registrar revealed that no application existed and the admission process had never been completed.

Following the discovery, multiple delays and repeated false assurances occurred. The intermediary claimed server maintenance issues and administrative backlogs, refusing to facilitate direct face-to-face verification.

Facts vs Claims

Distinguishing objectively verified and documented records from unverified personal interpretations.

Documented Facts

Financial Ledger Records Bank statements confirm two distinct transaction transfers totaling $7,700 to the intermediary's account.
Absolute Absence of Registration Written responses from the State University Registrar confirm no admissions, credit transfers, or applications were ever submitted.
Direct Written Directive WhatsApp message archives verify that the intermediary explicitly ordered the students to withdraw from their existing classes.

Opinions & Claims

Assumed Intentions Speculations regarding whether the intermediary planned this outcomes from the start, or simply suffered operational failures.
Third-party Rumours Local gossips or social rumors suggesting the intermediary has engaged in similar operations with other families in the area.
Speculative Destination of Money Assumptions regarding exactly how the $7,700 was spent, in the absence of verified bank trace records.

Lessons & Precautions

Essential warning checkpoints derived from this incident to protect other families and students.

Never Exit Previous Enrolments Prematurely

Do not withdraw from an active college until you hold the official, validated, and university-signed admission docket for the next institution.

Reject Verbal Promises Completely

Verbal statements and friendly relationships cannot substitute for official portal registrations. Always verify information through written reports.

Verify Directly with Universities

Contact the registrar’s admissions office directly via official website emails or public phone numbers to confirm receipt of applications and files.

Request Official Written Receipts

Every single tuition installment or fee deposit must yield an official cashier receipt generated directly by the university system.

Keep Meticulous Payment Records

Maintain precise bank statement records, references, chats, and date logs of all interactions. This serves as critical legal verification later.

Seek Legal Counsel Early

At the first sign of systematic deflection or refusal to provide verification, consult with licensed legal professionals to protect your interests.
